# ADR-2: Target Audience and Communication Approach

> **Scope**: Document decision clusters, not individual technology choices. Group related decisions that work together (e.g., "Frontend Stack" not separate ADRs for framework, styling, deployment).

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Date:** 2025-11-28
- **Feature:** 001-k12-ai-efficiency
- **Context:** The research paper needs to serve both academic rigor and practical utility for decision-makers. We needed to determine the primary audience and communication style that would ensure the research is both credible and actionable.

## Decision

- Primary Target Audience: Undergraduate economics students studying AI adoption in educational settings
- Secondary Audience: Education administrators evaluating AI adoption
- Writing Style: Academic tone with practical focus on ROI and implementation considerations
- Readability: Flesch-Kincaid grade level 10-12 to ensure accessibility
- Communication Focus: Balancing academic rigor with actionable insights for administrators

## Consequences

### Positive

- Paper will be accessible to the specified target audience of economics students
- Maintains academic credibility while providing practical value
- Clear focus on ROI and cost-benefit analysis for decision-making
- Appropriate readability level for educational administrators
- Dual audience approach increases potential impact

### Negative

- May not fully satisfy pure academic researchers looking for more technical depth
- Need to balance two different sets of expectations and needs
- May require additional effort to ensure content is appropriate for both audiences
- Risk of being too general to satisfy either audience completely

## Alternatives Considered

- Purely academic audience: Would target researchers but limit practical application
- Classroom teachers as primary audience: Would focus on daily workflow impacts but miss administrative decision-making perspective  
- Technology-focused audience: Would emphasize technical aspects but lose economic perspective
- General public: Would simplify concepts but diminish academic credibility
